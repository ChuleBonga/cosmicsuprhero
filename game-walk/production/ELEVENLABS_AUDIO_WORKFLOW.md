## Audio approval stages
1. Identify each **physical source** and its story reason to sound (device transport, actual repair activity, garments, footsteps, settlement soundscape), never generate generic space bleeps.
2. Estimate credits; only perform minimum needed original takes, logging generation ID and cost. Use no invented NPC dialogue until narrative canon and acting direction are accepted.
3. **Audition** with the physical action and animation in-engine or timed video; reject if fake, glossy, noisy, too long or inconsistent with the analog-world design.
4. Inspect duration, peaks, DC offset, leading/trailing silence, stereo/mono needs; edit and normalize for in-game use, guard autoplay restrictions.
5. Store original WAV/PCM source under source-control/media archive with rights and license; export web-friendly OGG/MP3 or another tested codec with accurate cue boundaries.
6. Wire via gated, accessible audio player and verify mute/unmute, interruption, mobile browser gesture and recovery. Obtain artist approval before public publishing.

## Voice policy
For NPC dialogue, establish the character's actual personality, motivations, language and approved lines first; then evaluate original voice performers or approved licensed voices. **Do not clone a private person's voice without permission** and do not use a generic synthetic voice to settle cultural identity. Supply accurate captions regardless of voice choice.
